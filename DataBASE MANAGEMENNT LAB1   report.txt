This task wasn’t as easy as breading or drinking water, we faced many challenges and difficulties the three most notable ones are: 

Readability: Putting all entities into a single diagram caused heavy overlap and made it hard to read. We split the design into 10 sub-diagrams (Person, Laboratories, Research Project, Budget, Equipment, Certifications, Reservations, Maintenance, Calibration, Consumables) and then merged them into one final diagram, leaving out some attributes so it stays readable on a slide.

Here’s a brief explanation of the modeling choices we made, we didn’t include everything just some notable ones: 

•Person (PE1–PE6): ISA hierarchies person → student/employee, employee → academic/administrative/technical, academic → dean/faculty. Dean and faculty are subclasses because they play a crucial rule later on having a relationship with other entities (RE3, LA4); scientist and postdoc were chosen to be values of the attribute role since they play no major role later on. reports_to is recursive relationship linking person with person. 

 

• Labs, projects, budgets (LA3, LA4, PR2–PR4, BD2, BD3): attached_to and involves are many-to-many, total on laboratory and project. role on involves gives one role per person-project pair (PR4). supervised_by and managed_by have key and total constraints (LA4, BD2). Funding uses two binaries, not a ternary (BD3). 

 

• Maintenance and calibration (MA2, MA3, CA1–CA4): both are weak entities of equipment_unit, with partial keys start-time and calibration-date. performed_by (key and total) links maintenance to technical staff. 

 

In defining the system boundaries, several assumptions were made by us, the members of the group project: 

• We first assumed that an equipment unit can only be present in exactly one laboratory. 

• We also assumed that student and employee are disjoinct subclasses of person meaning that a person cannot be a student and an employee at the same time. 

• We assumed that the dean could choose an approver from any person, meaning they can be a student or an employee. 

• We also assumed and argued over the fact that they can only be one dean. 

• We assumed in addition to what was cited previously that the cerial number is unique across all the equipment units meaning that it’s a priority queue. 

 

Here’s a list of the requirements that could not be expressed using the ER diagram: 

• PE5: the dean does not report to anyone; faculty and administrative staff must report to the dean, and there are no cycles. The diagram can only say each employee reports to at most one employee. 

• PR3: Each project has exactly one PI, at most one Co-PI, and both must be academic staff. 

• PR2 and RE1: administrative staff cannot join projects or make reservations. The diagram cannot exclude one subclass of a person from a relationship. 

• CE1 and CE7: Only holders of a valid, unexpired certification may reserve units of a model that requires one. This is a constraint across several relationships over time. 

• RE4 and EQ7: a unit must be reserved before use, and reservations of the same unit must not overlap.
